## 0. Read this first — what "exact replica" means here

The goal Darius stated: *"recreate this video exactly — every detail, cut, transition, sound, quality, voiceover — an exact replica of what I watched."* The thing to nail to the tea is the **production craft and the quality bar**: the doodle art style, the color system, the cut rhythm, the voiceover tone/cadence, the sound design, the hook, and the retention structure.

Do **not** re-voice the reference's narration word-for-word and re-upload its identical edit. That produces a duplicate of someone else's video — it gets buried as "reused content," can earn a copyright strike, won't monetize, and (the whole point) won't earn *its own* millions of views. It defeats the goal.

So: **clone the format and the quality exactly; write an original script in the identical structure, voice, and topic family.** This is literally our playbook (`product_vision_one_stop_shop` + the niche method: replicate the proven format, original angle). Done right, the output is indistinguishable in *look, feel, pacing, and quality* from the reference, while being our own video. That's the deliverable.

If Darius wants the same *subject* (the history of sleep), that's fine — write a fresh script on it. If he'd rather a different dominatable topic in the same mold, even better for a brand-new channel.

## 1. The production spec sheet (match these to the tea)

**Length & shape:** ~8–9 minutes. Single continuous essay, no hard chapter cards. One idea flows into the next.

**Visual style (the core thing to get right):**
- Crude, intentionally-simple **hand-drawn doodles** — single-weight black marker/felt-tip outlines, slightly wobbly (not vector-clean). MS-Paint-grade flat fills.
- **Stick figures**: round/oval white heads, simple dot or oval eyes, expressive eyebrows doing all the emotional work (worried, blank stare, angry, content). Bodies are literal stick limbs. No faces beyond eyes+brows+mouth.
- **Flat, saturated color fills**, no gradients/shading: purple couch, green lamp, blue phone, brown tree, orange fire, etc. Thick black hand outlines around everything.
- **Simple props/metaphor icons** drawn the same crude way: light switch, ceiling lamp with radiating yellow rays, bed, campfire, books/scrolls (prayer book, court record, diary), a streetlight, a crossed-out shield (= "no protection"), a long black bar = a timeline.
- Occasional **split-frame comparisons** (e.g. a 2×2 grid of sleepers; before/after; three labeled objects in a row).

**Color = emotion/scene key** (backgrounds are solid color, chosen for mood):
- **White** → explainer / diagram / "fact" beats (timelines, labeled objects, icon metaphors).
- **Royal/navy blue** → night, interiority, contemplation (person lying awake, thinking).
- **Orange + blue band** → sunrise / warmth / "natural rhythm."
- **Dark navy** → nighttime bedroom (moon in a window).
- **Brown + green** → outdoors / past / nature (tree, campfire on dirt under a starfield).

**On-screen text / captions:**
- **Hand-lettered ALL-CAPS** marker font (matches the drawings — not a system font). Examples seen: `EVERY THING YOU KNOW`, `EVERY. SINGLE. ONE.`, `HUMAN HISTORY`, `AWAKE / ASLEEP`.
- Appears on **emphasis beats only** — a 2–4 word punch tied to the narration, not every line. It's a *hook/retention* device, not a subtitle track.
- **Small lowercase labels under objects** for "evidence" beats (`prayer book 1400s.`, `court record 1400s.`, `Diary, 1400s.`, `1500s.`).
- **Red annotations**: a red **circle** scrawled around a key word/object, and a red **arrow** pointing at the thing that matters (classic explainer callout). Used sparingly for "look here."

**Cut cadence & motion (measured from the reference):**
- **~2–3 seconds per drawing.** A new drawing every couple of seconds. Across 8.5 min that's **~180–220 distinct hand-drawn images**. (Our 80-frame scan under-counted; the true rate is one drawing every 2–3s — build to that.)
- Drawings are mostly **static, held 2–3s**, with either (a) a very subtle slow **push-in / Ken-Burns zoom**, or (b) a tiny animated element (light rays shimmer, fire flickers, a hand moves the switch, thought-dots rise). Not full character animation — it's "moving illustration."

**Transitions:** Overwhelmingly **hard cuts** between drawings. Occasional fast **black wipe** (frame goes black between scenes, e.g. a portrait-on-a-wall beat). No fancy 3D/whip transitions. Keep it clean and quick.

**Voiceover (the spine):**
- **Calm, measured, articulate** male, **warm-neutral American** accent, mid pitch. Documentary essay tone — **no hype, no upspeak, no "hey guys."**
- ~**140–150 wpm**, unhurried, even level. Slows slightly and lowers on the emotional/philosophical beats for weight.
- Sounds like high-grade narration (clean TTS or a calm human read). **Matching the *voice character* matters more than which engine** — Darius explicitly OK'd not using ElevenLabs if another voice matches better. Pick the voice that lands this exact tone (see §3).

**Music & SFX:** ⚠️ **Confirm by ear from the reference before finalizing** (our analysis was frames + captions, no audio waveform). Format convention for this style: a **very soft ambient pad or no music bed at all** — the VO carries it — plus **sparse diegetic SFX** (faint fire crackle on the campfire beat, light night ambience). Do **not** over-score it; busy music breaks the contemplative tone. If a bed is used, keep it well under the voice (~ -18 to -22 dB feel).

**Format/quality:** **1920×1080, 16:9, ~30fps.** Clean, crisp export — the drawings are simple but the *file* is sharp (no compression mush, no letterboxing). Loudness-normalized voice, consistent level throughout.

## 2. The writing formula (so the original script matches the reference's effect)

The reference follows a **"melancholy history-of-an-everyday-thing" essay arc.** Reuse the *structure and devices*, write your own sentences:

1. **Cold-open hook (0:00–0:25):** a present-tense "you" statement that reframes something mundane as strange/lost. Pattern: *"Right now, [ordinary thing about your life]. Your ancestors never had this."* → immediately a huge-time-scale stat (e.g. "for 300,000 years, every human who ever lived…"). No intro, no channel name. Drop straight into the claim.
2. **The thesis of loss:** "we gave something up and never noticed." Plant the open loop: *what* did we lose? (Don't answer yet.)
3. **Historical evidence stack:** concrete, specific, dated artifacts/records that prove the claim (the reference uses 1400s prayer books, court records, diaries; a 1500s text reference circled in red; a 16th-century physician). Specificity = authority.
4. **The vivid reconstruction:** paint the lost world warmly (campfires, the visible Milky Way, the quiet window between two sleeps). This is the emotional core.
5. **The mechanism (the "smart" payoff):** the science of *why* — cause→effect chain in short declarative sentences (light → retina → brain reads daytime → melatonin delayed → rhythm disrupted; the camping-resets-melatonin study). This is what makes it feel authoritative, not fluffy.
6. **The reframe / philosophical turn:** flip a "problem" into "your body remembering." Pattern: *"What if [the thing we call a disorder] isn't a malfunction — it's [the old design] reasserting itself?"*
7. **Quiet close (no CTA hype):** a short, resonant final line that lands the loss. The reference ends on essentially *"we traded all of it for a light switch, and most of us never knew it was gone."* End on the emotion, fade. (A soft "subscribe"/next-video nudge can go in the description or a 1-line end, but the reference keeps the *video* clean.)

**Retention devices to copy:** the present-tense "you," the giant-timescale numbers, the open loop held until the mechanism, dated specifics, the AWAKE/ASLEEP-type visual punch on key claims, and the rule that **on-screen text only appears on the line you most want them to remember.**

**Length target:** ~1,300–1,500 words of narration ≈ 8–9 min at this pace.

## 4. Exact-match tuning notes (where our current output tends to drift)

- **Don't let the image model "beautify" it.** Our naturalist/technical presets render *detailed* art; this reference is deliberately *crude*. The prompt must enforce simplicity ("childlike marker doodle, crude, simple") or it won't match.
- **Crude ≠ low quality.** The *drawings* are simple; the *export* is crisp 1080p. Keep resolution/quality high.
- **Captions are hand-lettered, not a font overlay.** Bake the caption into the image prompt (`render the caption reading exactly "X"`, ALL-CAPS, marker style) — same mechanism proven on the bird/B58 captions — rather than compositing a system-font subtitle. Only on emphasis beats.
- **Background color must change with the beat's mood** (white/navy/orange/brown). If every beat is white it won't feel like the reference.
- **Cut rhythm is faster than our recent videos.** ~2–3s/image, ~190 images. If beats run 5–8s it'll feel slow vs the reference.
- **Red circle/arrow callouts** on 2–4 "evidence" beats add a lot of the reference's flavor — include them in those beats' prompts.
- **VO must be calm + American**, not the British George we default to for longform — re-audition.
